# How the canon teaches jazz voicings & free accompaniment

_Research findings for [#3](https://github.com/niclaeysthomas-ctrl/piano-dojo/issues/3) · part of the Wayfinder map [#1](https://github.com/niclaeysthomas-ctrl/piano-dojo/issues/1) · feeds the roadmap [#2](https://github.com/niclaeysthomas-ctrl/piano-dojo/issues/2)._

**Scope.** The skill Thomas wants — chord symbols → rich two-hand voicings + free right-hand embellishment + reharmonization, "play the chords beautifully and go with the flow." This surveys how the authoritative methods sequence, teach, and drill that skill. (Not bebop single-line soloing, which most of these treat as a separate track.)

## Sources surveyed

- **Mark Levine, _The Jazz Piano Book_** (Sher Music, 1989) — the reference text; exact 23-chapter order taken from the publisher's contents PDF.
- **PianoGroove** (Hayden Hill) — the *Essential Jazz Harmony* 7-step syllabus + the graded course index (Lead Sheet Fundamentals → Essential Harmony → Elegant Solo → Advanced Harmony), plus a dedicated chord-substitution/reharmonisation course.
- **Berklee: _Berklee Jazz Piano_** (Ray Santisi, Berklee Press) — 18-chapter method distilling the college curriculum.
- **Open Studio** (Peter Martin, Adam Maness) — *Jazz Piano for Beginners* + *Jazz Chords for Beginners*.
- **Jazzedge** (Willie Myette) — the "Beginner's Roadmap to Jazz Piano."
- **Hal Leonard: _Jazz Piano Method_ Bk 1–2** (Mark Davis) — a mainstream graded method.
- Cross-checks: Piano With Jonny ("10 Steps Beginner→Pro"), Learn Jazz Standards (reharmonization taxonomy).

## The canonical progression (consensus of all six)

The methods agree on the spine below. Ordering is near-identical; they differ mainly on *how early rhythm/feel/ear enter* (see Divergences).

**0. Fundamentals — scales, intervals, triads, the 5 seventh-chord qualities, ii–V–I.**
The shared entry point (Levine ch.1–2; PianoGroove step 1; Jazzedge "fundamentals"; Jonny steps 1–3; Davis Bk1). *Why first:* everything downstream is spelled from these. *Drill:* the five 7th-chord qualities and the major ii–V–I around the circle of fifths. *Time:* prerequisite; assume Thomas mostly has this.

**1. Shell / guide-tone voicings (3-note: root+3rd+7th, or 3-7 rootless).**
The single most-cited "beginner→intermediate hinge." *What:* strip a chord to the notes that state its quality (3rd + 7th, the guide tones). *Why here:* smallest voicing that sounds like jazz, and it teaches voice-leading (3rds/7ths of a ii–V–I move by half-step). Levine ch.3 "Three-Note Voicings"; Jonny step 4; Jazzedge "R7 shells"; Open Studio opens on "Shell Voicings"; PianoGroove folds shells into step 1. *Drill:* ii–V–I shells in all 12 keys, then on a real tune ("Moon River"). *Time:* a few weeks.

**2. Extensions — 9 / 11 / 13, added _on top of_ the guide tones.**
*What:* color tones above the 7th. *Why here:* they enrich a structure you can already voice — the order (guide tones first, colour second) is deliberate. Levine ch.5 is literally "**Adding** Notes to Three-Note Voicings"; PianoGroove step 2; Jonny step 5. *Drill:* extended ii–V–I in all keys, applied to standards ("Misty," "Tenderly"). *Watch:* the "avoid" 11 over major chords. *Time:* weeks–months.

**3. Rootless voicings (Bill Evans "A" and "B", 4-note left-hand voicings).**
The crux for Thomas's goal. *What:* drop the root (a bassist/left hand covers it), voice 3-5-7-9 ("A") or 7-9-3-5 ("B") in a sweet register around middle C. *Why here:* the modern comping standard; two interlocking forms let the top voice step smoothly through a progression. Levine ch.7 "Left-Hand Voicings" (+ ch.8 altering them); PianoGroove step 3 (paired with stride so the left hand can *self-supply* the root when playing alone); Jonny step 6; Jazzedge "rootless." *Drill:* alternate A/B through ii–V–I so the top note moves by step; then over standards ("Body & Soul"). *Time:* the intermediate plateau — months of daily work to make fluent in 12 keys.

**4. Alterations (♭9 ♯9 ♯11 ♭13) + tritone substitution.**
*What:* tension tones on dominant chords; the tritone sub swaps a V7 for the dom7 a tritone away. *Why here:* dominant colour once the plain rootless shapes are automatic. Levine introduces tritone sub early as a *device* (ch.6) but full altered voicings at ch.8; PianoGroove step 5; Jonny step 5. *Drill:* altered ii–V–I; hearing each alteration. *Time:* months.

**5. Quartal / "So What" voicings (4th-based chords).**
*What:* stacks of 4ths — open, modal, harmonically ambiguous (the "So What" sound). *Why here:* an alternative to tertian stacking; needs a secure harmonic ear first. Levine ch.12 "So What Chords" + ch.13 "Fourth Chords"; Santisi ch.9 "5-part fourth voicings"; Jonny step 9. *Time:* intermediate.

**6. Upper-structure triads / slash & polychords.**
*What:* voice a complex (usually altered) chord as a plain major/minor triad in the right hand over guide tones in the left. *Why here:* a *system* for the altered sounds of stage 4, and the conceptual bridge to reharmonization. Levine ch.14 "Upper Structures"; PianoGroove step 6; Santisi ch.10; Jonny step 10. *Drill:* memorise which triad sits over which dominant. *Time:* intermediate–advanced.

**7. Block chords / drop-2 / chord-melody (locked-hands, Shearing/Powell/stride).**
*What:* harmonise the *melody note-for-note* — melody on top, chord beneath, two hands moving as one. This is the "solo, no bassist" skill. Levine ch.16 "Voicings, Voicings, Voicings," ch.17 "Stride & Bud Powell," ch.19 "Block Chords"; Santisi ch.8 open voicings, ch.11 larger voicings, ch.12 approach-note harmonisation; Jonny steps 7–8 (block + drop-2); Davis Bk2 locked-hands/drop-2. *Time:* 1–3 years to sound musical.

**8. Reharmonization.**
The active-rewriting capstone. Typical device order (Learn Jazz Standards; PianoGroove chord-sub course; Jazzedge "Theory/Reharm"): diatonic substitution → secondary dominants → **tritone sub** → inserting/back-cycling ii–V's → diminished passing chords → modal interchange / borrowed chords → chromatic-bass & pedal points → parallel/non-functional. *Why last:* you must own the original changes before bending them. *Time:* multi-year to do fluently by ear.

**9. Right-hand freedom & comping feel (threaded throughout, not bolted on last).**
Embellishment (grace notes, enclosures, chromatic approach, turns, filling the gaps between melody phrases) and comping *rhythm* (syncopation, swing, space). Santisi ch.5 "Syncopation" and ch.6 "Comping in Rhythm" sit early; Levine ch.21 "'Comping." Open Studio (Peter Martin) is emphatic that *listen-and-imitate, groove, and swing-feel come first* — before harmony is piled high. Ear + feel + repertoire run in parallel to every stage above (Levine has you *sing* intervals from ch.1; Open Studio ends its beginner run on ear training).

## Consensus & divergences

- **Strong consensus on the harmonic spine:** fundamentals → shells → extensions → rootless → alterations → quartal/upper-structures → block/chord-melody → reharm. Levine, PianoGroove, Santisi, Davis, Jazzedge and Jonny all trace essentially this line.
- **Rootless voicings are the pivotal unlock** everyone flags as the beginner→intermediate boundary.
- **The real divergence is where _time/feel/ear_ enters.** The book methods (Levine especially) are harmony-first and say little about groove; the video schools (Open Studio, Jazzedge) are *feel-first / blues-first / listen-and-imitate*, inserting comping rhythm and swing **before** the harmony gets dense. For Thomas's target — sounding like Simon and "going with the flow" — the feel-first camp matters: voicings without time and touch don't land.
- **Tritone sub recurs:** taught early as a voicing device, then again as a reharmonization tool.

## Common beginner traps (synthesized)

1. **Root-position 7ths with the root doubled in both hands** → muddy, no voice-leading. The shell/rootless stages exist precisely to break this.
2. **Learning voicings as hand-shapes, not by function** → can't transpose or connect them. Antidote: drill through all 12 keys and alternate rootless A/B so the top voice steps.
3. **Extensions before guide tones are automatic** → mud, and the "avoid" 11 on major. Order matters (Levine literally "adds notes to" existing 3-note voicings).
4. **Voicing too low** (below ~C3) → muddiness; rootless voicings want the sweet register around middle C.
5. **Harmony-hoarding with no rhythm/feel** — the Open Studio critique; comping groove is its own skill.
6. **Reharmonizing before playing the plain changes cleanly** — reharm is a capstone, not a shortcut.
7. **Neglecting the ear** — every method threads ear training so you can *hear* the colour you're reaching for.

## Rough time-to-competence (practitioner estimates — hedge; not hard-sourced)

- Shells + comp a standard with guide tones in 12 keys: **weeks → ~2 months.**
- Fluent rootless A/B comping in all keys (the intermediate plateau): **~6–18 months** of consistent practice.
- Quartal + upper-structures + block-chords/chord-melody: **1–3 years.**
- Fluent on-the-fly reharmonization + free RH embellishment (the "free accompanist" tier #2 targets): **multi-year.**

## Implication for the roadmap (#2)

The six methods hand us an almost off-the-shelf phase order for competences ① (voicings) and ③ (reharm): **shells → extensions → rootless → alterations → quartal/upper-structures → block/chord-melody → reharm.** The design decision the sources *don't* settle for us — and where Thomas's "go with the flow" goal pushes hard — is **interleaving ② right-hand freedom and ⑤ ear/feel from Phase 1 rather than appending them**, per the Open Studio / Jazzedge feel-first stance.
